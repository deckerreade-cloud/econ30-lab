# Research Memo Draft

## Title
Commercial Real Estate Office Cycles and Distributional Effects

## Research Question
How do boom-and-bust cycles in office markets redistribute risk, wealth, and
opportunity across institutional investors, small business tenants, and urban
workers?

## Motivation
Commercial real estate is one of the largest asset classes in the economy.
Large swings in occupancy, rents, values, and financing conditions can shift
economic burdens unevenly across stakeholder groups and may amplify inequality.

## Scope
- Geographic focus: San Francisco and New York
- Asset focus: office segment
- Channels:
  - Asset valuations and REIT exposure (institutional)
  - Rent and demand pressure (small business tenants)
  - Employment sensitivity to office-market stress (urban workers)

## Data Inputs
- Vacancy rates
- Office property value proxies
- Rent indices
- Cap-rate proxies
- Office/CRE REIT performance
- Urban employment proxies

## Empirical Strategy
1. Build a city-time panel with harmonized frequencies and units.
2. Identify boom-bust episodes via drawdowns, vacancy shocks, and cap-rate expansion.
3. Construct stakeholder impact indices and compare dynamics across cities.
4. Use dashboard scenario shocks to illustrate directional redistribution effects.

## Preliminary Interpretation Framework
- Falling office values can reduce investor wealth while also easing rent pressure
  for some tenants.
- Elevated vacancy can coincide with weaker local labor demand, disproportionately
  affecting workers tied to office-centric urban ecosystems.
- Net distributional effects depend on balance-sheet exposure, bargaining power,
  and labor-market flexibility.

## Next Steps
- Integrate public source data feeds in place of synthetic inputs.
- Add robustness checks for alternative cycle definitions.
- Expand city sample beyond SF/NYC for external validity.
