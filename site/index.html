Who wins and loses when office values crash?

After the pandemic, declining office demand redistributed risk and opportunity: hurting many small businesses and downtown workers while creating openings for institutional capital. This story moves from the national picture to San Francisco and New York — then asks what comes next.

The national picture

Office became the distressed heart of CRE: record vacancy prints, value drawdowns, and a refinancing calendar that keeps pressure on owners and lenders. The headline numbers are large — but the pain is not evenly shared.

Micro-recession, macro debate

Some analyses frame the shock as concentrated in office and certain cities rather than a single economy-wide collapse. That matters for who loses first — and who can wait, or buy, through the cycle.

Academic work on work-from-home and office values points to long-run repricing and hundreds of billions in destroyed office value nationwide — with 46% long-run decline in NYC office values in a leading lease-level study.

San Francisco: extreme vacancy and a long fiscal shadow on downtown.

Case study: San Francisco

Tech-heavy remote adoption hit San Francisco early and hard. Market reports document sustained stress; policy voices argue recovery requires deliberate action — not just market time.
